## 1 — S1.a, the inorder traversal

> S1: write `my-length` and the inorder traversal with an accumulator. Follow skills/scheme-dev/SKILL.md.

- **Unit:** the inorder traversal and the function that prints it.
- **Produced:** `code/20_q3.rkt` (new file) — the tree representation and the
  height, leaf and visiting-order conventions documented in the file header,
  `inorder-onto`, `print-inorder`.
- **Commit:** `feat(S1): inorder-onto accumulator traversal and print-inorder`

**`my-length` was asked for here and deliberately not written.** S1 needs no
list length: the traversals thread an accumulator, the height uses `max`, and
the leaf count adds. `my-length` is required by S2.b and belongs in
`20_q4.rkt`, where it was written. Adding it to `20_q3.rkt` would have left
dead code in the file.

## Notes on the decomposition

The inorder traversal came first because it establishes the pattern the other
two reuse: the accumulator that replaces the forbidden `append`, and the fact
that the list is assembled from its right-hand end backwards, so subtrees are
threaded in the reverse of the order they are visited in. Once that was
checked against the worked examples, the preorder and postorder traversals
were the same idea with the `cons` in a different place.

The prompt that was deliberately not asked: "write 20_q3.rkt", or any prompt
covering all three of S1.a, S1.b and S1.c at once.
